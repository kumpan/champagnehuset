## Industry, in one line

ChampagneHuset sits in **premium / luxury wine retail & importing**, more precisely the
**grower-champagne specialist** niche (FR: *champagne de vignerons*) — a curated e‑commerce +
importer/distributor model serving consumers (via Systembolaget), restaurants/hotels (HoReCa),
and private import. Think **"specialty fine-wine merchant"** crossed with **"luxury DTC brand."**

## How to make this inspiration search sharper

A few suggestions to get more out of this round:

1. **Split "competitor" from "inspiration."** A/B above are who you *compete with*; C/D are
   who you *learn craft from*. Judge them differently — don't copy a Krug brand site's UX into
   a shop that needs a fast cart.
2. **Score each on specific axes**, not vibes. Suggested columns: navigation/IA, product page
   depth (dosage, grape %, vintage), storytelling, photography, mobile, checkout friction,
   gifting flow, HoReCa/B2B path, language/i18n. A simple 1–5 grid surfaces patterns fast.
3. **Capture screenshots**, not just links — sites change and Awwwards entries disappear.
4. **Look outside wine.** The best luxury DTC patterns live in fashion, fragrance, watches,
   spirits (e.g. niche whisky/mezcal brands). Worth a second list if useful.
5. **Mind the Swedish context.** Systembolaget is the consumer sales channel, so your site's job
   is discovery/brand/education + B2B, not consumer checkout. Weight competitors that solve the
   same "shop you can't buy from directly" problem (the house brand sites in D do exactly this).
6. **Note what to *avoid*** as you browse — cluttered merchant sites, slow image-heavy luxury
   sites — an anti-pattern list is as useful as the inspiration list.
